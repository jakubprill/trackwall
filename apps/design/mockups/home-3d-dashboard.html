A 3D dashboard as the first screen

One screen that answers three questions when Trackwall opens: what waits for me, what state the repos are in and what runs now. The middle is a 3D scene drawn with Pawn, and the sides show the same numbers in plain HTML, so the scene hides nothing. Round two: three scene proposals with richer objects in the Trackwall style, short labels with a preview on hover, and clicks that lead to sessions and repos.

    Switch between the three proposals with the A, B and C buttons above the scene. Hover an object for a preview. A click shows details on the right, and a double click, Enter or the button in the details goes to the session or repo (the mockup only says where it would go). Drag to rotate. The objects are built from simple solids; in the app they would be small glTF models loaded through Pawn (Model, Assets).

    Three scene proposals chosen: A, built as the Orbit layout (ADR-33)

    All three have the same panels (Needs you on the left, Details on the right), the same set of objects and the same label rules. They differ in what sits at the centre of attention.

    A · Orbit+

    Rings, extended

    The daemon core is a reactor with a turning ring of slots; sessions are drones on an inner track, tied to their repos; repos are modules on the rim. Connections are thin wires that packets travel along: HTTP faster, packages slower.

    B · Floor

    Control hall

    The “Layers” version, turned into an isometric view: four layer zones on a dotted floor, pipes routed at right angles, sessions above their repos, session wires meeting at the daemon in front. It shows the structure best.

    C · Track

    Work track

    A glowing track through the stations Working, Needs you, Failed, Review, Done (and Proposed, once there is propose_task). A session stands at the station of its state and moves on when the state changes. Repos are only a shelf in the background, so this is a view of sessions, not of the environment.

    Labels: the bare minimum

    • Repo: the name only, and only on a repo that is not clean. A clean repo has no label until you hover it.
    • Session: one word, only when action is needed (Needs you, Error). Working sessions speak through colour and motion.
    • Zones and stations: the name of the layer or stage.

    Hover, click, double click

    • Hover: a preview card at the cursor with the state, the sessions in the repo, the connections and a hint of what a click does; the connections light up.
    • Click: details on the right and the camera moves closer.
    • Double click, Enter or the button in the details: go to the session terminal, the change review or the repo. In the app that is real navigation to the existing views.

    The rule for the whole screen: the scene is a second way of showing the same numbers, not the only one. Everything you need to read is also in the HTML panels. So the dashboard works with a screen reader, with WebGL off (the dashboard without the scene, with the panels only) and in a narrow window.
