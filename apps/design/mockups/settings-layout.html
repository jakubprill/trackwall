A new Settings layout: three proposals

Settings was one column of seven sections, with more on the way. Below: an inventory of what is there and what is coming, one grouping shared by all variants, and three mockups. The mockups are clickable where changing a category shows something.

The problem is not only the length. The column mixes three different things: settings changed once a month, state you glance at (config backup, updates), and diagnostics you open when something does not work (errors, doctor). On top of that, half of the diagnostics live in a separate Daemon view. Splitting the column into tabs does not fix that; the groups come first.

Variant A

Categories on the left

chosen

Like settings in VS Code or the operating system: a narrow list of categories, and on the right only the sections of the chosen category, as wide as today. A category with a problem (doctor, a new error) has a dot, and so does the Settings icon in the view bar. The Daemon view leaves the bar, because its content moves into Diagnostics and Agents.

Settings

Click a category. In a narrow window (with the supervisor panel widened) the category list folds into a horizontal bar above the content.

For
  • A short screen: at most four sections at once, and a new feature is a new item in a category, not a longer column.
  • Diagnostics apart from settings, and the dot says where to look.
  • Every category can have its own address, so an error notification opens Diagnostics directly.
  • Least new code: the Surface components stay, navigation is added.
Against
  • One more click to see something in another category.
  • Repo hosts as a category with one section looks a bit empty (it could go into Workspace for four categories).
Variant B

Two columns: settings and state

Still one scrolling page, but in two columns: on the left what you set, on the right what you watch. A sticky bar of jumps to the groups on top. The smallest change of habits.

Variant C

Overview with state and a details panel

On the left every item as a row: name, current value and a state dot. The doctor result on top. Clicking a row opens its form on the right. Settings becomes the “does everything work” screen along the way.

For

Recommendation

A, with one idea from C

Variant A: categories on the left, five groups, a dot on a category with a problem and a dot on the Settings icon in the view bar. From variant C only this: the doctor shows a link like “Fix in Repo hosts” next to a result and opens the right category. A full list with the state of every item costs a lot and gives little for a dozen items.

Against the “let us split it into tabs” intuition: horizontal tabs on top were rejected. Five categories fit, but tabs do not show state as clearly, and in a narrow window they turn into a scrolling bar anyway. A settings search is not worth it either: for a dozen items it is more code than use.

A move that needed approval: removing the Daemon view from the bar (Ctrl+9 and the macOS menu). Its table goes to Diagnostics, and the Copilot CLI connection to Agents. The bar has one icon less, and all diagnostics are in one place.

Not in this change: showing supervisor.changesCode and toolPermissions in the UI. Those are separate features with their own decisions (on purpose only in yaml today), so the mockup shows only where they would go.

Decided
1. Which variant?A, built (ADR-102).
2. Move the Daemon view into Settings?Yes; the Daemon view and Ctrl+9 were removed.
3. Repo hosts on their own or in Workspace?On their own, because cloning settings join them.
