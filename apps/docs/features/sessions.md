# Copilot CLI sessions

Run several Copilot CLI agents side by side, each in its own repo, and always know which one is waiting for you.

![Copilot CLI sessions and agent tasks in a grid, some working, some waiting for you or asking for approval](/screenshots/sessions-grid.png)

## What you get

- Several Copilot CLI sessions at once, all in one window: in a repo's own working copy, or as free sessions in your projects folder, tied to no repo.
- A state for every session: working, waiting for you, asking for approval or idle, a warning when a working one [may be stuck](/features/while-you-were-away), and what its conversation used: AI credits, or premium requests on the older billing, with the sum of all listed sessions.
- A nudge when an agent needs you: a flashing taskbar icon with the number waiting, a system notification and an entry in the tray.
- A grid of terminals, so you can watch up to nine agents at once on a wide monitor.
- Sessions that keep running when you close the window, and come back with their conversations after Trackwall restarts or updates.

## How it works

Open "Sessions", press "+" and pick where the agent works. With a repo and the "Own worktree" pill on, your prompt becomes an [agent task](/features/tasks) on its own branch. Turn it off and "Start session" opens Copilot CLI in the repo's own folder, the working copy your editor uses; pick "No repo: the projects folder" instead and it opens a free session in your projects folder, which can look at every repo. Either way the prompt is optional: what you write there is the agent's first message, otherwise you type in its terminal. The pills under the prompt work as for a task: "Model: default" takes a Copilot CLI model id (with your own model provider it suggests the provider's models, see [mise environment](/features/mise-environment)), "Chat (preview)" shows the session as a conversation instead of a terminal, and in a Java repo "Java navigation" gives the agent a [language server](/features/java-navigation). The session keeps them when you restart it and after Trackwall restarts. "No approvals" is not there: a session without its own worktree always asks before its tools, because nothing reviews what it changes. Copilot CLI starts in a terminal inside Trackwall, already connected to the [Trackwall MCP server](/features/mcp). Start as many sessions as you like; the list on the left shows each one with its repo (or "Free session"), its state and what the agent is doing right now.

A session without its own worktree changes your files directly: there is no "Changes" tab, review, "Verify" or merge request, which only a task has. The form says so, and warns you when another session already works in the same folder, because two agents in one working copy change each other's files and switch its branch under each other.

A new session shows "waiting for you" as soon as Copilot CLI is ready for your first message. When an agent finishes its turn, its session shows "waiting for you" again; when it asks for approval of a step or a plan, "asking for approval" or "waiting for plan approval". Either way the counter on the view bar goes up. The session stays waiting until you have looked at it: select it, or type into its terminal, and it becomes "idle". In the grid, only the selected tile counts as looked at. A request for approval keeps waiting until you answer it. "Next waiting" (or Alt+J) takes you straight to the next session that needs you.

You do not have to keep Trackwall in front. When its window is in the background, a waiting session shows a system notification ("Session in orders-api: Copilot CLI is asking for approval"), the taskbar icon flashes (the Dock icon bounces on macOS), and the tray menu gets a "Waiting: orders-api" item. While anything waits for you, the Trackwall icon on the taskbar (in the Dock on macOS) shows how many sessions and task proposals are waiting, and the tray icon has a dot. Win+Alt+J (⌃⌥J on macOS) works from any app: it brings Trackwall forward on the next session that needs you. A left click on the tray icon opens a small panel with everything waiting for you, where you can also [answer an approval request](/features/approvals) without opening the session.

The switch above the terminal changes the layout: 1 shows the selected session with its full panel, 2 and 4 show pages of that many terminals, and Auto fits the whole list on screen, up to nine at a time (six sessions on a 34-inch ultrawide are three columns and two rows). Every terminal, alone or in a tile, sits on the same background as the rest of the window. The selected tile has the accent ring and takes the keyboard; a tile waiting for you has a warning frame. A task's tile shows in its header what needs you. While the task's mise config waits for your trust (the agent changed it, or it was never trusted), a yellow "Trust mise" chip stays there; click it to read the changed files and press "Trust this config", or open the worktree in VS Code. A verification that runs, passed or failed has a chip too. Everything else the task has to say is a badge: yellow with the number of warnings (such as files another task also changes), grey otherwise. The badge and the verification chip open the task's sections over its terminal, and Esc closes them. A warning that comes up while you watch the grid lies over the terminal as one line, with "Show" and an X. The ⋯ button opens the task menu: its branch, port, model and when it started, and VS Code, Verify, Stop or Resume, "Changes", which opens the task alone on its changes, and "Details". In a narrow tile the header shortens the task's title and shows a working, waiting or ended state as its coloured dot only, with the text in the tooltip. A session that got variables from mise shows a key with their number; hover it for their names, or click it to keep the list open until you click again or press Esc. The key turns yellow when the session got less than its place gives, for example from a config that is not trusted yet. None of these change the terminal's size.

Sessions that belong together are easy to tell apart. When a session hands work to a task, and that task hands work further, they form a group: the main session with everything under it. Each group of two or more has its own colour, shown as a stripe along its rows in the list and along the top of its tiles. Next to a name, ↳1 or ↳2 says how many levels it sits under the main session, and a tile says what it sits under ("under payments"). To watch one group by itself, hover one of its rows and click "⊞ group". The grid switches to Auto with only that group, and a chip in the group's colour on the layout bar shows the filter. Clear it with its ×, or select something outside the group. A session alone has no colour, and the yellow, green and red of the states keep their meaning.

Every session also gets short instructions from Trackwall on how to work with you: a session in a repo hands larger work over as a [task proposal](/features/main-session), and a session of an [agent task](/features/tasks) stays in its own worktree, does not push and finishes with a summary. They also tell the agent which Trackwall it runs in and where this documentation is; [Session instructions](/features/session-instructions) has the details.

Under its state, every session that has talked to the model shows what its conversation used so far, the way Copilot CLI's `/usage` counts it: AI credits, or premium requests if your plan still bills those, or only tokens when the model comes from your own provider. Hover it for the number of model calls and tokens. The task's header shows the same for its session, and under the "Sessions" heading you see the sum of all listed sessions. A resumed session or task keeps counting its conversation from the first prompt. The number appears a moment after a turn ends, since Copilot CLI writes it down in batches. What every task, session and tool used in a whole week is in [Copilot usage](/features/copilot-usage).

## Use it

1. Install Copilot CLI and sign in to it.
2. Open "Sessions" (Ctrl+3, ⌘3 on macOS).
3. Press "+", pick a repo (or "No repo: the projects folder"), turn off the "Own worktree" pill, pick a model, Chat or Java navigation if you want them, and press "Start session".
4. Work in the terminal as you would in any Copilot CLI session.
5. When a session waits for you, press Alt+J to jump to it.

## Shortcuts

| Keys | What happens |
|---|---|
| Alt+1 … Alt+9 (⌥ on macOS) | Select the session at that position in the list, turning the grid to its page. |
| Alt+J | Jump to the next session or task proposal waiting for you, from any view. |
| Alt+0 | Open the [supervisor](/features/supervisor) panel, from any view. |
| Win+Alt+J (⌃⌥J on macOS) | Bring Trackwall forward on the next session waiting for you, from any app. |
| Ctrl+PageDown / Ctrl+PageUp (⌘⇧] / ⌘⇧[ on macOS) | Open the next or previous tab of a task, such as "Shell" from "Terminal" and back; in "Repos", of a repo. |

Alt+1 … Alt+9, Alt+0, Alt+J and the tab keys work while a terminal has the focus too, and are not typed into the session; PageUp and PageDown alone still scroll Copilot CLI; Esc and Ctrl+C go to Copilot CLI as usual and interrupt the agent's turn. On macOS, an Option key that types a character on your keyboard layout (⌥5 for `[` on a German keyboard) stays a character.

## Good to know

- Copilot CLI must be on your `PATH` as `copilot` and signed in.
- To switch a running session to another model, press **Model** in its header, type or pick the model and press "Restart on this model". The session starts again with the same conversation on that model and keeps it when you restart it later. This is the way to change the model with your own [model provider](/features/mise-environment), where `/model` offers only the model the session started on.
- When Copilot CLI runs a session on another model than the one you asked for, a strip under its header says so, for example "Runs on gpt-6-luna, not claude-sonnet-5.5". Copilot CLI before version 1.0.109 does that without an error when your account does not offer the model, and any version after `/model`. The strip shows once Copilot CLI has answered on the model it took, so it can appear a few seconds after the start. Press **Model** to restart the session on the one you want.
- When the model does not answer, a strip under the session's header says "The model call failed" with Copilot CLI's message, for example `Execution failed: Model "gpt-9" is not available.` From version 1.0.109 that is what happens to every prompt when your account does not offer the model: the turn ends without an answer. The session then shows an error and waits for you, like a session that finished its turn, until you look at it. Press **Model** to restart the session on a model you have. The strip goes away once a call goes through.
- Trackwall stops a session only for a task with a [token budget](/features/tasks), set in "Settings > Agents". Other sessions it never stops; to cap one, type `/limits set max-ai-credits <number>` in it (at least 30).
- Each session gets the variables and tokens of your mise config, the line under its header says which, and in the grid a key next to its title lists them: see [Environment from mise](/features/mise-environment).
- A free session runs in the projects folder, so the trusted folder you set for Copilot CLI and the antivirus exclusion of that folder cover it. It can change any repo there, and it can hand work over as a [task proposal](/features/main-session), but it does not run a repo's mise tasks or services through Trackwall. Unlike the [supervisor](/features/supervisor), you can run several, and it may run commands and change files.
- The [supervisor](/features/supervisor) is not one of the numbered entries: its heading tops the list with the work it delegated under it, and selecting it opens its panel on the right.
- Copilot CLI takes over the mouse in the terminal. Drag over text to select it and press Ctrl+C or right-click to copy it, as the hint under the selection says. You can also drag with Option (macOS) or Shift (Windows) held down, then copy with Cmd+C or Ctrl+C. Ctrl+C without a selection interrupts the agent. Paste with Cmd+V or Ctrl+V (Ctrl+Shift+V works too). While text is selected, links in the session open only after you clear the selection with a click. Copilot CLI's `/copy` copies the agent's last answer, also when Trackwall runs in a container.
- Ask the agent to copy something ("copy that command for me") and a strip under the session's header shows what it wants to copy, its first line and how many lines and characters. "Copy" becomes clickable a second after the text appears or changes, and nothing changes on your clipboard until you click "Copy"; "Dismiss" drops it. A newer request replaces one you have not copied yet.
- Session terminals use Trackwall's own colours and have no background of their own, so Copilot CLI's text, highlights and diffs blend into the rest of the window. To hide Copilot CLI's tab bar and start-up tip, see the [supervisor](/features/supervisor) page.
- Drag a file or folder from Explorer or Finder onto a terminal to paste its path, quoted when it has a space. When Trackwall runs in a [dev container](/guide/dev-container), or you use it in the browser, a dropped file is copied to Trackwall first and the terminal gets the copy's path, so the agent can open it there; folders cannot be dropped this way.
- Press Ctrl+Shift+F (Cmd+F on macOS) in a terminal to find text on its screen and in what scrolled away: Enter goes to the match above, Shift+Enter to the one below, Escape closes the search. A web address the program printed, such as the one a service logs when it starts, opens in your browser on Ctrl+click (Cmd+click on macOS).
- Coming back to a session (from another view, another session or another layout), its terminal shows the screen you last saw at once and brings it up to date as soon as Trackwall answers. Whenever a terminal waits for Trackwall for a moment, the first time too, "Connecting to the session…" shows in its corner; keys you type meanwhile go to the session once it is connected.
- "Stop" ends the session with everything it started; "Close" removes an ended session from the list. The X next to a session does both in one click and asks first while the agent is working. On an [agent task](/features/tasks), the X closes only its session: the task stays on the list; select it and press "Resume" to start a new session in its worktree. Once the task has no session, its X ends the task: it asks, in the tile or under the row, whether to keep the branch and remove the worktree or remove both, and the task leaves the list.
- When Trackwall quits, restarts or updates, its sessions stop, and the next start opens again every session that was running then, the [supervisor](/features/supervisor) and task sessions included, with the same conversation. An agent that was in the middle of a turn waits for you in the reopened session: ask it to go on. Sessions you stopped yourself stay stopped. What waited comes back with them: task proposals you have not started or rejected, review comments and messages a task's agent has not read, news the supervisor and the sessions in your repos have not read about the work they handed over, each task's last verification, and the agents a mise configuration turned away, which still hear when you trust it; a reopened session that has something unread is asked to read it. Sessions are not recorded.
- Trackwall follows a session's state without starting a program at each step of the agent, so an antivirus that scans every program start does not hold the agent up. Copilot CLI itself loads its package (over 200 MB) from `%LOCALAPPDATA%\copilot\pkg` on Windows at every start. If your antivirus skips only your projects folder, move the package there: run `setx COPILOT_PKG_CACHE_HOME "<projects folder>\.copilot-pkg"`, then Quit Trackwall from the tray and start it again. Copilot CLI unpacks itself there on its next start, and the folder does not show up as a repo.
- Behind a proxy, Trackwall adds `127.0.0.1` to `NO_PROXY` for its sessions, so the agent reaches Trackwall's tools on your computer directly. Programs the agent runs see that value too.
- When something waits that a session's agent has not read yet (a review, a message, news about work it handed over), a strip under the session's header says "Unread by the agent", since when, and what holds it back. When nothing would send it on its own, for example after you stopped the agent with Esc or in a session you just started, **Deliver now** types the request into the empty input box. See the [supervisor](/features/supervisor) page for how these requests reach the agent.
- The notifications appear under "Trackwall" in the notification settings of Windows and macOS, where you can turn them off.
- The layout lasts until Trackwall closes; an extra window keeps its layout across restarts.
